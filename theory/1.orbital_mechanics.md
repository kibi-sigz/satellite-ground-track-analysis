# 01 — Fundamentals of Orbital Mechanics

## 1. Introduction

Orbital mechanics is the branch of physics and aerospace engineering that studies the motion of artificial satellites and other objects under the influence of gravitational forces.

A spacecraft in orbit continuously falls towards Earth under gravity. However, its tangential velocity allows it to keep moving around Earth instead of falling directly onto its surface.

The motion of a spacecraft can be described using a set of orbital parameters called **orbital elements**.

## 2. Basic Orbital Geometry

A satellite generally follows an elliptical orbit around Earth.

Two important points define the shape of an elliptical orbit:

- **Perigee:** The point where the spacecraft is closest to Earth.
- **Apogee:** The point where the spacecraft is farthest from Earth.

The distances from Earth's centre to these points are:

\[
r_p=R_E+H_p
\]

\[
r_a=R_E+H_a
\]

Where:

- \(R_E\) is Earth's mean radius.
- \(H_p\) is perigee altitude.
- \(H_a\) is apogee altitude.
- \(r_p\) is perigee radius.
- \(r_a\) is apogee radius.

## 3. Semi-Major Axis

The semi-major axis represents half the longest diameter of an elliptical orbit.

It is calculated as:

\[
a=\frac{r_p+r_a}{2}
\]

For Variant 7:

\[
R_E=6371\text{ km}
\]

\[
H_p=700\text{ km}
\]

\[
H_a=800\text{ km}
\]

Therefore:

\[
r_p=6371+700=7071\text{ km}
\]

\[
r_a=6371+800=7171\text{ km}
\]

And:

\[
a=\frac{7071+7171}{2}
\]

\[
\boxed{a=7121\text{ km}}
\]

## 4. Orbital Eccentricity

Eccentricity describes how much an orbit deviates from a perfect circle.

It is calculated using:

\[
e=\frac{r_a-r_p}{r_a+r_p}
\]

For Variant 7:

\[
e=\frac{7171-7071}{7171+7071}
\]

\[
\boxed{e\approx0.007021}
\]

Since eccentricity is close to zero, the orbit is nearly circular.

The general classification is:

- \(e=0\): Circular orbit.
- \(0<e<1\): Elliptical orbit.
- \(e=1\): Parabolic trajectory.
- \(e>1\): Hyperbolic trajectory.

## 5. Orbital Inclination

Inclination is the angle between the orbital plane and Earth's equatorial plane.

It is represented by:

\[
i
\]

For Variant 7:

\[
\boxed{i=80^\circ}
\]

This means the spacecraft travels in an orbital plane inclined by 80 degrees relative to Earth's equator.

## 6. Right Ascension of the Ascending Node (RAAN)

RAAN is the angle measured in Earth's equatorial plane from the reference direction to the ascending node.

The ascending node is the point where a spacecraft crosses the equatorial plane while moving from the southern hemisphere towards the northern hemisphere.

RAAN determines the orientation of the orbital plane around Earth.

For five spacecraft in Variant 7:

| Spacecraft | RAAN |
|---|---:|
| SC-1 | 0° |
| SC-2 | 72° |
| SC-3 | 144° |
| SC-4 | 216° |
| SC-5 | 288° |

The spacecraft therefore occupy five differently oriented orbital planes.

## 7. Argument of Perigee

The argument of perigee is the angle measured within the orbital plane from the ascending node to the perigee direction.

It is represented by:

\[
\omega
\]

It determines the orientation of the elliptical orbit within its orbital plane.

The value used in a simulation must be obtained from the assignment or explicitly stated as a modelling assumption.

## 8. True Anomaly

True anomaly describes the spacecraft's angular position along its orbit, measured from the perigee direction.

It is represented by:

\[
\theta
\]

Unlike RAAN and argument of perigee, true anomaly changes continuously as the spacecraft moves around Earth.

## 10. Conclusion

The orbital parameters determine the size, shape and orientation of a spacecraft's orbit.

These parameters form the foundation for calculating orbital motion, transforming spacecraft coordinates and generating ground tracks.
