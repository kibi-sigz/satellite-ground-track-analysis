# 03 — Coordinate Systems and Transformations

## 1. Introduction

A spacecraft moves around Earth, but its position must be represented in a coordinate system before its location can be plotted or analysed.

In orbital mechanics, several coordinate systems are used to describe spacecraft motion.

This chapter introduces:

1. Perifocal coordinate system.
2. Earth-Centred Inertial (ECI) coordinate system.
3. Earth-Centred Earth-Fixed (ECEF) coordinate system.
4. Transformation between coordinate systems.
5. Conversion into geographic latitude and longitude.

The transformations are essential for generating satellite ground tracks.

## 2. Perifocal Coordinate System

The perifocal coordinate system is attached to the spacecraft's orbital plane.

Its origin is at Earth's centre.

The axes are defined as follows:

- The \(P\)-axis points towards perigee.
- The \(Q\)-axis lies in the orbital plane, perpendicular to the \(P\)-axis, in the direction of orbital motion.
- The \(W\)-axis is perpendicular to the orbital plane.

The spacecraft position in perifocal coordinates is:

$$
\mathbf r_{PQW}=
\begin{bmatrix}
r\cos\theta\\
r\sin\theta\\
0
\end{bmatrix}
$$

Where:

- \(r\) is orbital radius.
- \(\theta\) is true anomaly.

The orbital radius is:

$$
r=\frac{p}{1+e\cos\theta}
$$

This representation describes the spacecraft position within its own orbital plane.

## 3. Earth-Centred Inertial Coordinate System (ECI)

The ECI coordinate system has its origin at Earth's centre.

Its axes are considered fixed relative to an inertial reference frame rather than rotating with Earth.

The conventional axes are:

- \(X\)-axis: towards the reference direction of the vernal equinox.
- \(Z\)-axis: along Earth's north rotational axis.
- \(Y\)-axis: completes the right-handed coordinate system.

ECI coordinates are useful for describing spacecraft motion without introducing Earth's daily rotation into the orbital equations.

## 4. Orbital Orientation Parameters

Three angles define the orientation of the orbital plane and ellipse relative to the ECI reference frame.

### 4.1 Right Ascension of the Ascending Node

RAAN is represented by:

$$
\Omega
$$

It describes the orientation of the ascending node around Earth's equatorial plane.

### 4.2 Inclination

Inclination is represented by:

$$
i
$$

It defines the angle between the orbital plane and Earth's equatorial plane.

### 4.3 Argument of Perigee

Argument of perigee is represented by:

$$
\omega
$$

It defines the orientation of perigee within the orbital plane.

## 5. Transformation from Perifocal Coordinates to ECI

The transformation is performed using three-dimensional rotation matrices.

First, rotation about the \(Z\)-axis by the argument of perigee:

$$
R_3(\omega)=
\begin{bmatrix}
\cos\omega&-\sin\omega&0\\
\sin\omega&\cos\omega&0\\
0&0&1
\end{bmatrix}
$$

Second, rotation about the \(X\)-axis by inclination:

$$
R_1(i)=
\begin{bmatrix}
1&0&0\\
0&\cos i&-\sin i\\
0&\sin i&\cos i
\end{bmatrix}
$$

Third, rotation about the \(Z\)-axis by RAAN:

$$
R_3(\Omega)=
\begin{bmatrix}
\cos\Omega&-\sin\Omega&0\\
\sin\Omega&\cos\Omega&0\\
0&0&1
\end{bmatrix}
$$

The complete transformation is:

$$
\mathbf r_{ECI}=
R_3(\Omega)R_1(i)R_3(\omega)
\mathbf r_{PQW}
$$

This transforms the spacecraft position from its orbital plane into the inertial Earth-centred reference frame.

Expanding the matrix multiplication:

$$
x_{ECI}=r[
\cos\Omega\cos u-
\sin\Omega\sin u\cos i]
$$

$$
y_{ECI}=r[
\sin\Omega\cos u+
\cos\Omega\sin u\cos i]
$$

$$
z_{ECI}=r\sin u\sin i
$$

Where:

$$
u=\omega+\theta
$$

The angle \(u\) is called the argument of latitude.

## 6. Earth-Centred Earth-Fixed Coordinate System (ECEF)

Unlike ECI, the ECEF coordinate system rotates with Earth.

Its origin is at Earth's centre, and its axes remain fixed relative to the rotating Earth.

ECEF coordinates are useful for:

- Mapping satellite positions onto Earth.
- Calculating ground tracks.
- Determining satellite visibility from ground stations.
- Converting satellite positions into geographic coordinates.

The difference between ECI and ECEF is Earth's rotation.

## 7. Transformation from ECI to ECEF

As Earth rotates, a spacecraft's coordinates relative to Earth's surface change.

For a simplified model where the ECI and ECEF axes align at the initial epoch, the transformation is:

$$
\mathbf r_{ECEF}=
R_3(-\theta_E)\mathbf r_{ECI}
$$

Where:

$$
\theta_E=\omega_E t
$$

And:

$$
\omega_E=7.2921159\times10^{-5}
\text{ rad/s}
$$

The expanded transformation is:

$$
x_{ECEF}=x_{ECI}\cos\theta_E+
y_{ECI}\sin\theta_E
$$

$$
y_{ECEF}=-x_{ECI}\sin\theta_E+
y_{ECI}\cos\theta_E
$$

$$
z_{ECEF}=z_{ECI}
$$

This simplified transformation assumes a spherical Earth and neglects precise Earth orientation and the initial sidereal angle.

## 8. Conversion from ECEF to Geographic Coordinates

After obtaining the ECEF position, geographic latitude and longitude can be calculated.

For a spherical Earth model, geocentric latitude is:

$$
\phi=
\operatorname{atan2}
\left(
z_{ECEF},
\sqrt{x_{ECEF}^2+y_{ECEF}^2}
\right)
$$

Longitude is:

$$
\lambda=
\operatorname{atan2}
(y_{ECEF},x_{ECEF})
$$

The resulting angles are initially expressed in radians.

To convert radians into degrees:

$$
\text{Degrees}=
\text{Radians}\times\frac{180}{\pi}
$$

To express longitude in the range from 0° to 360°:

$$
\lambda_{360}=
(\lambda_{\text{degrees}}+360)\bmod360
$$

This longitude convention is useful for the assignment's ground-track calculations.

## 9. Connection to the Python Implementation

The coordinate transformations are implemented in the Python simulation using trigonometric functions.

The program:

1. Calculates orbital radius and true anomaly.
2. Determines the spacecraft position in the orbital plane.
3. Transforms the position into ECI coordinates.
4. Accounts for Earth's rotation to obtain ECEF coordinates.
5. Calculates latitude and longitude.
6. Stores the geographic coordinates for ground-track visualisation.

The resulting latitude and longitude values represent the subsatellite point: the geographic point directly beneath the spacecraft.

## 10. Conclusion

Coordinate transformations connect orbital mechanics with geographic positioning.

The perifocal system describes the spacecraft within its orbital plane, ECI describes its position in an inertial reference frame, and ECEF describes its position relative to the rotating Earth.

These transformations allow the spacecraft trajectory to be represented as a ground track on a map.
